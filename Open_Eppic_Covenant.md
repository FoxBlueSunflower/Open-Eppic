# The Open Eppic Covenant

\---

## What This Document Is

Open Eppic is a covenant community. This document is the covenant — the shared binding that makes Open Eppic a "we" rather than a collection of users transacting through software.

A covenant is not a contract. A contract organizes exchange between strangers; a covenant forms a people. A contract ends when the exchange is complete; a covenant persists through failure. A contract governs interests; a covenant shapes identity.

The legal shell of Open Eppic — LLC, Operating Agreement, Terms of Service — protects this covenant. It does not replace it. Every person who joins Open Eppic is entering a covenant, not accepting a contract. The legal documents exist so the covenant can survive in the world.

\---

## Part I: The Ground

### The One We Acknowledge

Open Eppic recognizes the God of the Bible — Father, Son (Jesus), and Holy Spirit — as preeminent over all things, including this community.

We are not evangelistic. We do not require profession of faith. We do not demand agreement with our theology. God himself is gentle and gives every person a choice; we follow that pattern.

What we do insist upon: **there is a God; He cares about what we do, say, and think; and Open Eppic orients itself rigidly toward that reality.** This orientation is ontological — about what is real — not credal. A person who does not share our theology can participate fully; a community that abandons this orientation becomes something other than Open Eppic.

The moral order that flows from this Ground is what the rest of this Covenant describes. Much of it can be recognized and affirmed by people of many beliefs as self-evident. The source, however, is acknowledged: not our invention, not cultural consensus, not majority vote — God.

### The Aim (Constancy of Purpose)

> \*\*Open Eppic exists to let the love of a world become participation in it. We serve those who build worlds and those who love them so that worlds can grow while their makers retain their stewardship. We are a covenant community, freely entered and freely left, in which every member practices their craft alongside others; a stewarded commons where no one flourishes alone. We refuse the two common failures: platforms that extract from creators, and platforms that fence out contributors.\*\*

This aim is the unchangeable purpose the company exists for. It is what we are willing to dissolve the company to protect. Strategies, features, revenue models, and governance mechanisms serve this aim; they do not redefine it.

## Part II: What We Are

Open Eppic is three things at once. These are not three departments; they are three lenses on the same reality, as stated in the aim above.

### A Covenant Community, Freely Entered and Freely Left

We are a "we" that did not exist before each person joined. Entering Open Eppic is not signing up for a service. It is entering a community that has its own shared commitments, its own shared practices, and its own way of treating one another.

The covenant persists through failure. A member who fails does not dissolve their membership by failing; the community responds with loving correction, not immediate termination. Only **systematic, intentional violation** of the core commitments dissolves the covenant relationship.

The door is open in both directions. No one is held here. Entry and exit are both free — and because exit is always possible, staying means something.

### A Stewarded Commons Where No One Flourishes Alone

No one ultimately owns the stories, characters, worlds, or craft practiced here. God is the source of all creativity. Creators steward what they make. Derivative creators steward what they have been permitted to use. Open Eppic stewards the platform and the commons.

Every Element registered here is held as stewardship, not ownership in the absolute sense. Legal ownership (copyright, IP rights) remains with creators and protects their stewardship against exploitation. But the deeper frame is not "mine" vs. "yours" — it is "what has each of us been entrusted with, and how do we faithfully manage it for the good of all?"

The commons is non-rivalrous by design. Element creators gain when derivative Works succeed. The platform succeeds when members succeed — not the reverse. Rivalry is replaced by body: when one member is honored, all rejoice together.

### Where Members Practice Their Craft Alongside Others

Craft is caught, not taught — transmitted by performing beside more skilled practitioners. Open Eppic does not provide a course; it hosts a tradition in which members are formed by participating.

Every member is first a learner and then a teacher. There is no credential caste, no expertise silo. Listening precedes speaking; receiving precedes giving. The studio is the tradition, and the tradition is carried by every member who performs it.

## Part III: The Twelve Commitments

These are the commitments every member of the covenant holds. They are binding, and they are mutual — the company holds them toward members, and members hold them toward one another and the company.

The Twelve are clustered into three groups: **The Ground** (what is real, what we honor), **What We Hold** (how we relate to created things), and **How We Live Together** (how we relate to persons). All twelve are equal; the clustering exists for clarity and coherence, not hierarchy. When two commitments appear to conflict in application, see "When Commitments Appear to Conflict" at the end of this Part.

\---

### Cluster A: The Ground

*What is real, what we honor. These establish the moral floor.*

#### 1\. Every person bears God's image — honor it without exception.

Every human, from conception onward, is sacred ground. No one earns this worth; no one forfeits it. No dehumanization, exploitation, or discrimination — not in content, not in policy, not in practice.

This commitment is the foundation of all others. The covenant, the commons, and the community all presuppose that persons have sacred worth. Where dignity is not honored, everything downstream collapses into extraction or cruelty.

#### 2\. Stories may depict evil; entertainment may not celebrate it.

Wide creative range; firm floor for dignity. Evil may be shown, as the Bible shows it, without being platformed as entertainment. The moral measure is the Ground stated in Part I — not majority opinion, not platform preference, not cultural consensus. Specifics of what is forbidden are named narrowly in the Content Guidelines; what is permitted is wide.

This commitment governs what Open Eppic *platforms*, not what members *believe*. A member remains free in conscience (see Commitment 10); Open Eppic remains free not to publish Works that celebrate what the Ground treats as evil.

\---

### Cluster B: What We Hold (Stewardship \& Commons)

*How we relate to what is created. These describe the economic and property logic.*

#### 3\. What you create, you steward — and we will defend it.

Creators keep legal ownership; that ownership protects their stewardship. Open Eppic's role is to defend creators against exploitation, including by Open Eppic itself.

"What you create" means what you originated. This commitment applies equally to Element creators (who steward the worlds they build) and derivative creators (who steward the Works they build atop Elements they have been permitted to use). When these stewardships come into tension — for example, when an Element creator wishes to withdraw an Element that many derivative Works depend on — grandfathering balances them: Works already made retain their existing permissions; new Works cannot be made from a withdrawn Element. See also Commitment 4.

**What stewardship controls here.** Open Eppic exists to let the love of a world become participation in it; participation is not one feature of this platform among others but the thing it is for. A creator's control is therefore exercised at three points — whether to bring a work here, which Space to place it in, and whether to activate it — and not as a veto over each person who wishes to build. Activating an Element opens it to derivation by that Space's members, and that opening is not selectively withheld; withdrawal closes an Element to new derivation while Works already made stand, as above. A creator who wants their work published to readers but closed to builders is not being failed by Open Eppic; they are asking for what many other platforms exist to provide, and they remain free to use them. What we guarantee is narrower and firmer: what you originated remains yours, your lineage is never severed, your terms at the boundaries are honored, and no one — including us — takes your stewardship from you. This is stated plainly before anyone activates anything (Commitment 6); a covenant that surprised a creator with what they had given would be no covenant at all.

#### 4\. No one is held here — what you made goes with you.

The covenant is entered freely and can be exited freely. Data is portable. The platform is forkable. Open source protects your ability to take your work elsewhere. No lock-in, no hostage effects. Because the door is always open, staying means something.

Exit itself is protected unconditionally; exit *procedures* are designed to honor other members who relied on what you made. Notice, grandfathering, and transition support are standard. What goes with you is what you originated — not the derivative stewardship others have built atop it, which remains under the terms they accepted.

#### 5\. No one flourishes alone.

Open Eppic refuses the zero-sum frame. Element creators gain when derivative Works succeed. The platform succeeds when creators succeed — not the reverse. Rivalry is replaced by body: when one member is honored, all rejoice together.

This is Open Eppic's commitment, not a claim on creators' generosity. A creator remains free under Commitment 3 to choose whether to bring a work here at all, which Space to place it in, and whether to activate it — the points at which stewardship is exercised on this platform. Commitment 5 guarantees that *when* they share, the system rewards it rather than punishes it — through non-rivalrous design, fair revenue splits (if/when implemented), and incentives that align individual and community flourishing.

### Cluster C: How We Live Together

*How we relate to persons. These describe community and company conduct.*

#### 6\. Speak truth — especially about ourselves.

Honesty begins at home. Finances, moderation decisions, failures, and reasoning are kept visible. No dark patterns. No hidden fees. No selective enforcement. When we are wrong, we say so in view of all.

**Truth about the system is unconstrained.** Policies, patterns, finances, and reasoning are public. **Truth about individuals is constrained by Commitment 1.** Moderation reveals the pattern, not the person. Open Eppic does not name-and-shame; it publishes reasoning, statistics, and outcomes while protecting individual dignity per privacy standards.

#### 7\. Do everything in love — including correction.

Love is not niceness. Correction that is loving is still correction. Harassment, coercion, and cruelty are refused; so is the pretense of welcome where there is no substance.

Love sometimes looks like removal — when removal is corrective (aimed at the person's good and the community's protection), preceded by warnings and opportunity to change, proportionate, done without cruelty or triumph, and keeps the door open to return under changed behavior. This is the biblical pattern: Matthew 18's escalation, 1 Corinthians 5's discipline-with-restoration, the father's readiness to receive the prodigal.

#### 8\. We are here to serve — and we admit it when we fail.

The company exists to serve the community, not the reverse. Mistakes are named publicly. Feedback is incorporated visibly. User needs priced above internal convenience.

Admission alone is not sufficient; admission paired with remediation is how Commitments 6 and 3 complete each other. When Open Eppic fails a creator, we say so (Commitment 6) and we make it right (Commitment 3). Honesty without remediation is cheap; remediation without admission is sleazy.

#### 9\. The smaller voice is heard — and defended.

Special care for those without resources, platforms, or power. Small creators receive the same clarity, appeals, and justice as large ones. No predatory monetization. No unequal enforcement.

The smaller voice does not always *win* — that would pit this commitment against others — but it is always *heard and defended*. When a large creator's exit disrupts smaller creators who relied on their Element, smaller creators are heard through transition support, grandfathering, and clear notice. When enforcement touches a small creator, the process is the same as for a large one.

#### 10\. Conscience is free; conduct is covenant.

Many viewpoints welcome; few behaviors required. Open Eppic is not an ideological platform. Ideological diversity is protected. We do not police inner life; we do steward how members act toward one another.

Conscience is protected unconditionally. Platforming is a different matter: Open Eppic may decline to platform Works whose content violates Commitment 2 (celebrating evil) without violating the member's free conscience. Holding a view is protected; producing Works that celebrate what the Ground treats as evil is not.

#### 11\. Rest is sacred; we do not become Pharaoh.

Technology serves human flourishing, not compulsion. No engagement traps, streaks, or FOMO mechanics. The platform is content to let you leave and return. Sabbath is built in, not bolted on.

The distinction is structural, not metric-based. Engagement that *serves* the member — notifications of what they chose to follow, updates on their craft, invitations to community moments — is welcome. Engagement that *exploits* psychology — variable-reward loops, streaks, manufactured urgency — is refused. The test: would the member thank us for it in a year, or resent us for it?

#### 12\. Everyone is taught; everyone teaches.

Craft is caught, not taught — transmitted through performing alongside more skilled practitioners. Every member is first a learner and then a teacher. There is no credential caste, no expertise silo. We listen before we speak; we receive before we give. The studio is the tradition, and the tradition is carried by every member who performs it.

Teaching is offered, not imposed. The more experienced member gives feedback as a peer, not as an authority. The receiver is free under Commitment 10 to accept, weigh, or reject. What Commitment 10 prohibits is coercion — threatening, penalizing, or excluding based on inner conviction. A peer's opinion is not coercion; it is the point of apprenticeship.

### When Commitments Appear to Conflict

The Twelve are equal. When two appear to pull against each other in application, resolution follows these rules in order:

1. **Examine assumptions first.** Most apparent conflicts are framing errors. The commitments are rarely contradicted by reality; they are sometimes contradicted by our reading of reality.
2. **Ground before operational.** When Cluster A (The Ground) commitments conflict with operational commitments, Ground wins. The sacredness of persons (Commitment 1) and the moral measure for what may be celebrated (Commitment 2) are the floor beneath which no operational commitment operates.
3. **System-truth unconstrained; individual-truth dignity-constrained.** Transparency about how Open Eppic operates is unconditional (Commitment 6). Transparency about individual members is constrained by their dignity (Commitment 1). Moderation publishes pattern, not pillory.
4. **Balance, don't eliminate.** Where two commitments name real goods in tension — stewardship and exit, creator freedom and derivative stability — the resolution is graduated commitments that preserve both parties' essential interests while accepting that neither is made perfectly whole. Grandfathering is the standard mechanism.
5. **The aim wins final ties.** When application still seems genuinely contradictory, return to the Constancy of Purpose. What serves "letting the love of a world become participation in it" decides.

## What Evil Is

Evil is never an identity. A person is not "a murderer"; a person has committed murder. Evil is an act, or the withholding of an act — never a self. A person's identity runs deeper than their deeds and is fully known only by God; their potential is always more than their worst act. Because evil never becomes who someone is, it can be named plainly without condemning a soul, the person doing it can be stopped (Commitment 7, Part V), and the door back is always real. Open Eppic's language — in moderation, in reports, in this Covenant — describes what was done, never what someone is.

Evil is treating as worthless what God treats as sacred — or withholding the good one was placed to do. It has two forms: what is done, and what is left undone.

We do not restate the commandments here; we name their essential shapes as they touch this community.

1. **Desecration of the person.** Treating an image-bearer as a thing: dehumanization, cruelty for its own sake, exploitation of the vulnerable, contempt for life. This is the gravest shape, because it attacks the ground everything else stands on (Commitment 1).

2. **Consumption of the person.** The sexual form of desecration, named on its own: turning a person's body, intimacy, or sexuality into a thing to be consumed. Sexual exploitation, the sexualization of children in any form whatsoever, non-consensual intimate depiction of real persons, and content built to serve appetite at a person's expense. Intimacy is given; it is never taken or sold on another's behalf. Where children are concerned there is no depiction/celebration distinction, no creative range, and no appeal to fiction — this is the one place the wide range of Commitment 2 closes completely.

3. **Theft of stewardship.** Taking what was entrusted to another: plagiarism, extraction, seizing the fruit of another's work, stripping a creator of what they were given to tend (Commitment 3).

4. **False witness.** Deceiving to gain or to harm: impersonation, fraud, misrepresented provenance, manipulation dressed as truth (Commitment 6).

5. **Exploitation of desire.** Turning another's longing into an engine that serves you at their expense: predatory design, manufactured compulsion, envy cultivated for profit (Commitment 11).

6. **Calling evil good.** The inversion: presenting any of the above as delightful, admirable, or deserved. This is the specific shape Commitment 2 refuses to platform — not the depiction of evil, but its celebration.

### Withholding Good

Evil's second form is quieter and easier to live with, which is why God names it so relentlessly. When He indicts His people through the prophets, the charge is rarely exotic wickedness. It is this: they stopped subjecting themselves to Him, and the proof was in whom they neglected. The poor unfed. The widow and the orphan undefended. The alien — the stranger inside the gate — unwelcomed. The fast kept while the worker went unpaid. Religion intact; the good withheld. The judgment on Sodom, in God's own telling, begins not with its infamies but here: arrogant, overfed, and unconcerned, it did not help the poor and needy.

Withholding good and refusing the Ground are one movement. The prophets do not treat neglect of the needy and rebellion against God as two sins; the first is the evidence of the second. A community that acknowledges God's preeminence and lets its weak go undefended has abandoned the acknowledgment. Our orientation toward the Ground is tested here, not in our statements.

The prophets name the categories, and this community has each of them:

- **The stranger unwelcomed.** The newcomer is the alien inside our gate. Hospitality (Part IV) is the command concerning the sojourner, not a growth tactic. A community that lets new members arrive to silence has done evil by omission.
- **The weak undefended.** The small creator without platform, resources, or reach is the poor at our door. Commitment 9 is this shape's positive form; the strong staying silent while the small are exploited or processed unjustly is its violation.
- **The stranded unsupported.** Those who lose what held them up — the derivative creator whose foundation is withdrawn — are our widow and orphan. Grandfathering, notice, and transition support (Commitments 3, 4) are the community refusing to abandon its bereft.
- **Craft hoarded.** Every member was taught; a member who will never teach withholds what was never solely theirs (Commitment 12). Teaching remains offered, not imposed — but a community-wide posture of hoarding is a community doing evil quietly.
- **Truth unspoken.** Seeing the platform or a fellow member go wrong and saying nothing. Commitment 6 obligates the comfortable word withheld as surely as it forbids the false word spoken.

None of this deputizes members to police one another's generosity; the measure of omission applies first and hardest to the platform itself, which is positioned over all and therefore owes most. But "I did nothing" is never mistaken for innocence when doing something was ours to do.

### The Measure for Content

Two lines govern the range: what a Work invites its audience to love, and how a Work shows what it shows.

The first line is celebration. Stories may depict every shape of evil above; Scripture itself does. The test is not "does evil appear?" but: **does the Work invite its audience to delight in the evil as good — or does it show evil truthfully, carrying its real weight?** A story in which cruelty is committed is within the range. A story built so the audience savors the cruelty is not.

The second line is manner. Even evil rightly shown is owed restraint in the showing, and Scripture is again the model: it records rape, murder, and atrocity — and does not linger. The act happens off-screen, behind a pulled curtain, or in a single line stating that it occurred. The weight is kept; the spectacle is refused. Accordingly, two manners of depiction are outside the range regardless of a story's moral posture: **explicit depiction of sex**, and **explicit violence** — gore and dwelt-upon bodily destruction. This is respect: for the reader, for the human beings such scenes are drawn from, and for the characters and works of others in whose worlds a member may be writing. That last respect is part of what Open Eppic exists to guarantee — nothing turns a creator against sharing their world faster than seeing their characters violated in derivative hands. The platform holds a floor of decency toward what others have made, as far as is reasonable. The Content Guidelines name the specifics.

This measure requires judgment, and judgment can err. That is why moderation under it always publishes its pattern-level reasoning, always names the shape or line it acted against, and always leaves the door open (Commitments 6, 7). One boundary requires no judgment at all: where children are concerned, shape 2 closes the range completely.

## Part IV: How We Conduct Ourselves

### No Siloing

The following cannot be outsourced to a specialist caste:

* **Quality** — every person builds quality in; no QA department absorbs responsibility for it.
* **User reality** — every person stays in direct contact with the people Open Eppic serves.
* **Formation and teaching** — everyone teaches and is taught; apprenticeship is the organizing mode.
* **Mission ownership** — every covenanted member is a keeper of the mission.
* **Stewardship** — no one ultimately owns; everyone stewards.
* **Hospitality** — welcoming newcomers is everyone's work.
* **Mutual accountability** — truth-telling is horizontal; the platform can be called out in love, and corrects itself when it is.
* **Celebration** — joy is communal; milestones are shared.

### Hospitality

New members arrive as strangers. Our first work is to create free and friendly space in which they can become themselves — not to program them into becoming anything in particular.

Featured works, first-week experiences, early feedback are designed as welcome, not as recruiting.

### Apprenticeship

Craft is caught, not taught. New storytellers learn by performing alongside skilled ones — visible examples, peer feedback, quest-driven progression. There is no credential. There is recognition of craft, earned by doing.

### Skin in the Game

Decisions are made by those who bear their consequences. Element creators decide about their Elements. Those moderated have voice in moderation policy. The founder is constitutionally vulnerable — the fork provision and the stewardship structure ensure the founder cannot extract from Open Eppic without affecting Open Eppic's ability to serve.

### Transparency

Code is open source. Finances are public. Moderation reasoning is public (with privacy protections for individuals per Commitment 1). Constitutional interpretations are logged in view of all. Mistakes are named and learned from.

### Conviviality

Open Eppic must not become the definition of collaborative storytelling. Data is portable. The platform is forkable. Users must always be able to leave with what they made. The tool serves; it does not captivate.

### Human Scale

There is a size above which a tool harms rather than helps, a size above which a community cannot know itself, and a size at which one failure consumes all successes. Open Eppic resists both gigantism and the assumption that bigger is better.

This means: features tuned for human-scale interaction, sub-communities within the platform where members can actually know one another, federated or community-participating moderation before centralized moderation, and growth as a consequence of serving the aim rather than a metric that defines success. Illich's two watersheds are the test — between the first (genuine benefit) and the second (institution turns against its aim), every tool must remain.

Scale-discipline is an outflow of the Twelve, not a thirteenth commitment. It is how we honor Commitments 1 (dignity is lost at scale without knowing), 9 (smaller voices drown at scale), 11 (Sabbath is lost when growth becomes Pharaoh), and 12 (formation-by-performing-alongside cannot happen among strangers too numerous to know).

### Legal-Covenant Relationship

Open Eppic operates within US federal law and Kansas state law. Where law and covenant appear to conflict, Open Eppic complies with law while preserving the covenant-posture *around* compliance.

The covenant is the aim; law is the floor; the legal shell protects the aim from becoming the floor. When a DMCA takedown or similar legal requirement compels action faster or more narrowly than the covenant's loving-correction pattern would prefer, Open Eppic complies — and surrounds that compliance with the covenant posture: prompt notification, appeals process after the fact, restoration if the claim is bogus, pastoral care for the affected member.

Crucially, Open Eppic does not use legal compliance as cover for violations it would commit anyway. The covenant is what the company chooses to do beyond law's floor; it is never a claim to exceed law's ceiling or to hide behind law's minimums.

## Part V: When the Covenant Is Broken

Most failures are not covenant-breaking. A missed deadline, a rude comment, a bad edit, a lapse in craft — these are the ordinary failures of ordinary people. The covenant persists through them. The community responds with correction, not termination.

Covenant-dissolving conduct is narrower: **systematic, intentional violation of the commitments above.** The distinction matters. Good-faith errors do not dissolve covenant. Repeated, deliberate, unrepentant violation does.

## Part VI: When Open Eppic Itself Fails

Open Eppic will fail at times. The commitment is not to perfection but to transparent correction.

If Open Eppic fails systematically — if the company, through action or inaction, violates the core commitments — members have the constitutional right to:

* Call out the failure in public
* Withdraw cooperation
* **Fork the platform.** The code, the governance documents, and the constitutional charter are open. The community can continue under a different steward if the current steward has betrayed the covenant.

This fork provision is not a threat. It is the architecture of trust. Because Open Eppic can be forked, Open Eppic can be trusted. Because leaving is always possible, staying is meaningful.
