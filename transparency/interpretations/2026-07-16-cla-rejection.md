# Contributor Terms: DCO Adopted, CLAs Refused Permanently

**Date:** 2026-07-16

**Decision:** Code and content contributions to Open-Eppic require a
Developer Certificate of Origin sign-off (`git commit -s`) and nothing more.
Contributor License Agreements and copyright assignment are refused
permanently. Recorded in Locked Decision 17 (contributor terms) and Locked
Decision 18 (contribution policy) in MVP_Spec.md.

**Commitments implicated:** 3 (What you create, you steward — and we will
defend it), 5 (No one flourishes alone), 6 (Speak truth), and the Aim's
refusal of platforms that extract from creators. Code contributors are
creators.

**Conflict rules applied:** None — direct application.

**Reasoning:** A CLA grants the platform asymmetric rights over
contributions — typically the right to relicense, including to close the
code commercially — while the contributor retains no reciprocal power. This
is extraction, not optics: the contributor's stewardship of what they
originated (Commitment 3) is stripped at the door, and the mechanism exists
precisely so a platform can later profit from closing what was given openly
(violating Commitment 5 and the Aim). The pattern — glad, free contribution
early; exclusion from the fruit later — is part of what Open Eppic exists to
solve. It is therefore solved here first, in Open Eppic's own repository.

The DCO, by contrast, is a provenance certification only: the contributor
affirms the code is theirs to give under the project's license. It grants
the platform nothing beyond the license itself.

**Options considered:**

- **DCO — complies.** Provenance protection, zero asymmetry, no paperwork.
- **CLA — violates Commitments 3 and 5.** Rejected permanently, not merely
  deferred.
- **"Soft" relicensing clause inside the sign-off — violates by
  reclassification.** Any sign-off that grants relicensing rights is a CLA
  under another name; the size of the clause does not change the verdict.
- **Narrow unreachable-contributor clause — undecided, referred to counsel.**
  A clause triggered only by documented, prolonged good-faith outreach,
  constrained to copyleft-preserving destinations, and publicly logged could
  comply under conflict rule 4 (balance, don't eliminate — the grandfathering
  pattern). It still reserves a non-reciprocal right and strains Commitment 3
  in spirit; `AGPL-3.0-or-later` already covers the realistic need. It will
  be adopted only if draft legal language survives Covenant assessment, as
  its own logged entry.

**Decided by:** Founder, with advisory assessment.

**Consequences accepted:** Dual licensing and any relicensing outside the
AGPL family are foreclosed without per-contributor consent, permanently.
This foreclosure is deliberate: the platform accepts the same constraint it
asks contributors to accept. Because Open Eppic cannot quietly close the
code, Open Eppic can be trusted.
