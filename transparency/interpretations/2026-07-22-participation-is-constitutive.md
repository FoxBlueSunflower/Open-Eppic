# Covenant Amendment: Participation Is Constitutive — Commitments 3 and 5 Corrected

**Date:** 2026-07-22

**Decision:** Commitment 5's claim that a creator "remains free under
Commitment 3 to restrict their Element, set their own terms, or decline to
share" is corrected, and Commitment 3 gains a "What stewardship controls here"
paragraph. Together they state accurately what the platform's architecture
already requires: a creator's control is exercised in choosing whether to bring
a work here, which Space to place it in, and whether to activate it — not as a
per-derivation veto. Activation opens an Element to derivation by that Space's
members and cannot be selectively withheld; withdrawal is prospective, and
Works already made stand. There is no read-only publishing state, now or
planned. The Covenant is bumped to v0.4.

**Commitments implicated:** 3 (the amendment defines the shape of stewardship
control rather than reducing it), 5 (the inaccurate sentence originated here),
6 (the correction is itself required — a covenant that misstates what a member
is giving is a covenant that misleads), 4 (unaffected: what you originated
still goes with you), and the Aim directly.

**Conflict rules applied:** Rule 1 (examine assumptions). The apparent conflict
— creator freedom versus a commons that permits derivation — dissolved once
"freedom to restrict" was read correctly. The Covenant had described a
general-purpose publishing platform's freedom while Open Eppic is a
participation platform; the error was in the text, not in the architecture.
Rule 5 confirms the result: what serves "letting the love of a world become
participation in it" decides.

**Options considered:**

- **Amend the text to state the real scope of control — complies.** Adopted.
  The architecture was always this; the words were wrong.
- **Build a read-only publishing state to match the old wording — rejected.**
  It would make Open Eppic a lesser version of platforms that already serve
  that need well, and would hollow out the Aim. Creators wanting read-only
  publication are well served elsewhere, and remain free to go there.
- **Leave the text and accept a standing strain — rejected as violating
  Commitment 6.** The platform would have been publishing a promise of freedom
  it does not deliver. An inaccuracy about what a member gives is not a
  tolerable strain; it is a false witness by omission.

**Decided by:** Founder, with advisory assessment.

**Consequences accepted:** Open Eppic is explicitly not for creators who want
publication without participation, and says so. The narrowing is deliberate and
reduces the addressable audience. In exchange, the platform owes an affirmative
disclosure duty: the fusion of publishing and participation must be stated in
onboarding and at activation, before any creator invests. The remaining
guarantees — retained ownership of what you originated, unsevered lineage,
prospective-only withdrawal with grandfathering, and free exit — are
strengthened, not weakened, by being stated exactly.
