# Repository License: AGPL-3.0-or-later

**Date:** 2026-07-16

**Decision:** The Open-Eppic platform repository is licensed under the GNU
Affero General Public License, version 3 or (at the recipient's option) any
later version (`AGPL-3.0-or-later`). This is recorded as Locked Decision 17 in
MVP_Spec.md.

**Commitments implicated:** 4 (No one is held here — forkability), 6 (Speak
truth — code is open source), and the Aim's refusal of platforms that extract
from creators. Part VI (the fork provision) governs directly.

**Conflict rules applied:** None — direct application. The apparent tension
named in the original open decision (radical transparency vs. preventing
self-destructive give-away) resolved under rule 1 (examine assumptions): the
fork provision protects the community against the steward; it does not
obligate the steward to arm extractive third parties. A copyleft license
serves both goods at once.

**Options considered:**

- **AGPL-3.0 — complies.** True open source, written for network software:
  anyone running a modified version as a service must publish their
  modifications. Fork provision fully and immediately honored;
  extraction-resistant by force of law.
- **MIT / Apache 2.0 — strains the Aim.** Maximally open and maximally
  extractable: a funded competitor could close the code and out-market the
  covenant community. Donating infrastructure to potential extractors serves
  no commitment.
- **Fair-source (FSL-style delayed open) — violates Part VI in timing.** Code
  becomes open only after a multi-year window. A community betrayed in year
  one could not fork until year three. The fork provision's trust
  architecture ("because Open Eppic can be forked, Open Eppic can be
  trusted") depends on immediacy.
- **SSPL / dual licensing — deferred, not needed.** Reputational and
  administrative weight with no MVP benefit.

**"Or-later" reasoning:** AGPL Section 14 itself provides the
version-or-later option. Choosing it allows future migration to successor
AGPL versions without per-contributor consent, while the "out" is held by the
license's steward (the FSF) and constrained to licenses of the same copyleft
character — no platform-held relicensing power is created. A custom
"unreachable contributor" relicensing clause beyond this was considered and
referred to IP counsel; it will not be adopted unless draft language survives
Covenant assessment (see the CLA-rejection entry of the same date).

**Decided by:** Founder, with advisory assessment.

**Consequences accepted:** Everything server-side must be published — which
the Covenant already demands. Future relicensing outside the AGPL family
requires the consent of every contributor; the platform binds itself
alongside its contributors.
