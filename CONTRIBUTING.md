# Contributing to Open Eppic

Open Eppic is a covenant community. Contributing here is not transactional
labor-for-credit; it is participation in a shared work. Read the
[Open Eppic Covenant](./Open_Eppic_Covenant.md) first — it governs
everything, including this document.

## License and contributor terms

The platform is licensed **AGPL-3.0-or-later**. By contributing, you license
your contribution under the same terms.

**We use the Developer Certificate of Origin (DCO) — and we will never ask
you to sign a CLA.** A Contributor License Agreement grants a platform
asymmetric rights over your work; the Covenant names that extraction
(Commitments 3 and 5), and it is refused permanently. The reasoning is
public: see
[`transparency/interpretations/2026-07-16-cla-rejection.md`](./transparency/interpretations/2026-07-16-cla-rejection.md).

What this means in practice:

- Sign off each commit: `git commit -s`, which appends
  `Signed-off-by: Your Name <email>`. This certifies the code is yours to
  give under the project license — nothing more.
- Your contribution remains yours. You steward it; the license you granted
  is the license everyone gets, including us.
- **Privacy note:** DCO sign-offs place an email address in public git
  history. If you prefer not to expose a personal address, use GitHub's
  noreply address (`ID+username@users.noreply.github.com`) — enable
  "Keep my email addresses private" in GitHub settings. The contribution
  ledger itself carries no contact information.

## What contributions are accepted

Open Eppic's core is built quality-first (Deming): stable, deeply worked
systems over feature accretion. Contribution scope reflects that:

- **Core code** — accepted when aligned with the communicated build goals and
  current focus areas (see `Roadmap.md`). Unsolicited core features are
  generally declined, kindly; this is stewardship of the core's quality, not
  a judgment of the work.
- **Security** — welcome at all times, on anything. Report vulnerabilities
  privately first (see SECURITY.md once present; until then, contact the
  maintainer directly rather than opening a public issue).
- **Bug reports and reproduction cases** — among the most valuable
  contributions there are.
- **Documentation and onboarding writing** — hospitality is everyone's work.
- **Accessibility review** — every person bears God's image; the default web
  excludes some of them.
- **User-research participation** — interviews and journey feedback are
  contributions and are ledger-recorded as such.
- **Design and UX critique.**
- **Covenant scholarship** — the Covenant's Part VII lists open theological
  and governance work. Contributions there are as real as code.
- **Add-ons and integrations** — the open periphery. Feature energy is
  welcome here without core-scope constraints.

## Recognition and recompense

Every accepted contribution is recorded in the public, append-only
[contribution ledger](./transparency/contribution-ledger.md) — chosen public
name, contribution, date. No emails, no contact details.

On recompense, we promise exactly what we can keep, no more and no less:
contributors are heard, worked with openly, and publicly recognized now. If
and when Open Eppic's success creates value systems to share — monetary or
otherwise — the question of contributor participation in them will be decided
transparently, logged in the transparency log, with contributors heard. We
will not create a vague expectation and later hide behind never having
technically committed. The exact trigger and terms are an open decision,
tracked in MVP_Spec.md.

The same standard binds both directions: no contribution arrangement may
extract from the platform or its members, either.

## Conduct

The Covenant's commitments are the conduct standard: honesty (6), everything
in love including correction (7), the smaller voice heard (9), conscience
free and conduct covenanted (10). Correction here follows the Covenant's
pattern — warning, opportunity to change, proportion, an open door back.
